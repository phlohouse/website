# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Phlo is for data engineers, analytics engineers, platform teams, and technical founders who build lakehouse pipelines in Python. They are evaluating whether Phlo feels credible enough to trust with production data workflows, and whether it will fit their existing open data stack.

## Product Purpose

Phlo is the Python framework for building reliable, scalable data lakehouse applications. The website should explain that Phlo brings software-engineering discipline to lakehouse workflows: branching, quality checks, schema compatibility, lineage, promotion, and open-format interoperability.

Success means a technical visitor quickly understands what Phlo is, trusts the engineering taste behind it, and has a clear next step: read the docs, try the CLI, or inspect the project on GitHub.

## Brand Personality

Clear, fluid, exact.

The brand should feel precise and technical without becoming cold. It should use the flow motif as a disciplined metaphor for data movement, not as decoration. It should feel modern, optimistic, open, and quietly capable.

## Anti-references

Do not make Phlo look like a generic AI SaaS landing page: avoid decorative gradient blobs, oversized icon-card grids, vague startup copy, and ornamental polish that does not explain the product.

Do not make Phlo look over-editorial: avoid magazine-serif identity, gothic display type, broadsheet composition, and italic-heavy headings that fight the existing brand.

Do not make Phlo look like internal developer tooling: avoid dense terminal-first darkness, monochrome dashboards, or technical austerity that hides the warmth and motion of the brand.

Avoid decorative flow lines that cross or reduce the readability of important text.

## Design Principles

1. Practice what Phlo promises: the page should feel like a pipeline, with clear stages, visible handoffs, and understandable flow.
2. Show the product through concrete developer artifacts: code, checks, lineage, branch names, schema status, and open stack logos.
3. Use motion and color as structure: ribbons and strands should connect ideas or mark sections, not act as background noise.
4. Preserve technical trust: concise copy, crisp spacing, readable code, and stable alignment matter more than spectacle.
5. Keep the brand open and optimistic: bright surfaces, tinted neutrals, and vivid blue, teal, green, and amber should carry the visual identity.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast for text and controls. Preserve readable code samples, visible keyboard focus states, reduced-motion fallbacks, and color-independent status labels. Flow-line color should never be the only way to understand a relationship or status.
